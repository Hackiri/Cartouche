# 123. Cartouche manages the apps that are not games on screens of its own

2026-11-12

Cartouche manages the apps that are not games on screens of its own: they sit on the home's shelf after the games, as icon cartridges, with **Open** and **Details**; their sheet has **Open**, **App settings**, the language the app starts in and its orientation, LiveContainer's own settings of it, what the app says of itself, and **Uninstall**, which asks whether its data stays ([`CartoucheAppSheetView.swift`](../../launcher/Sources/CartoucheAppSheetView.swift), [`CartoucheAppSettingsView.swift`](../../launcher/Sources/CartoucheAppSettingsView.swift)). Such an app installs from **Import** as a game does, starts behind the console or in Cartouche's place as a game does, and sends nothing to the game measurements. The System page's **Open LiveContainer** and **Open SideStore** of 122 go: no page of LiveContainer's or SideStore's opens from Cartouche, as 24 has it. The pairing file's ownership (`CartouchePairing.claim`) stays, for Cartouche's own renewals and updates; patches 0024 and 0025 stay too, harmless to Cartouche's own flows and a fix of SideStore's launch should its pages ever show again.

**Why.** The player asked for Cartouche to have what SideStore and LiveContainer are for, installing and managing other IPAs, on Cartouche's own screens, not theirs (2026-11-12): SideStore's pages, opened from Cartouche, met its pairing prompts and device checks, which an app inside Cartouche never needs, since LiveContainer installs and signs it as it does a game; and LiveContainer's pages are LiveContainer's, not a console's. One shelf, not a second row: the D-pad model, the Duo layouts and the screenshots assume one.
